# Offering Machine Transformation Rules

## Emotional Engine

Choose one before generating:

| Engine | Spatial behavior |
|---|---|
| Comfort | Cradle, warmth loop, soft shelter |
| Celebration | Lift, burst, stage, orbit |
| Nostalgia | Memory capsule, replay ring, faded signal |
| Craving | Pull, circulation, drip, magnetic orbit |
| Reward | Pedestal, reveal, dispenser, crown |
| Companionship | Pairing loop, shared seat, echo chamber |
| Daily ritual | Repeating track, refill cycle, gentle clock |

## Source Map

Map at least two visible parts:

- Package, tray, cup, shell, or box → cradle or enclosure.
- Straw, handle, cable, spoon, or fold → conduit or route.
- Rim, crust, lid, label, or circular mark → orbit or interface.
- Steam, condensation, reflection, foam, or crumbs → signal or output.
- Repeated pieces → satellites or rhythm markers.

Do not invent an element solely to make the composition richer.

## Transformation Strength

Aim for 60–75% transformation. Preserve object identity and appetite while changing its role, scale, spatial behavior, and emotional meaning. A polished product photo is insufficient.

## Material Choice

- Food and drink: preserve edible surfaces; pair with pearl ceramic, soft enamel, cloudy resin, or satin metal.
- Cosmetics or small objects: preserve signature finish; pair with frosted acrylic, fabric, gel, or one restrained metal.
- Soft toys or fabric: preserve softness; pair with milky resin, woven mesh, paper, or translucent membrane.

Never convert every surface into clear glass or chrome.

## Prompt Skeleton

Include: object preservation, emotional engine, part-to-function map, one apparatus gesture, hero dominance, material palette, lighting, and explicit exclusions.

Example: "Keep the egg tart and milk tea immediately recognizable and appetizing. Make them a celebration-and-comfort Offering Machine: the tart rim becomes one warm orbit, the cup and tray become a soft pedestal, and the straw becomes a single circulation conduit. Hero occupies most of the frame; pearl ceramic, pastry, and a small satin-metal accent; milk white, tea green, candied coral, butter yellow; bright diffuse studio light. No shrine, flowers, random symbols, dense machinery, dark cyberpunk, excessive chrome/glass, or ordinary product retouching."

## Rejection Tests

- Blur the details: if the hero no longer dominates, simplify.
- Remove the apparatus: if nothing meaningful changes beyond styling, rebuild the concept.
- Trace each mechanism to the source: if it has no origin, remove it.
- Check appetite and warmth: if food looks synthetic or the object feels ominous, revise materials and light.
- Swap in a different object mentally: if the machine would look the same, strengthen object-specific behavior.
