# B.B.BAM Visual System

## Core Thesis

Do not put a style on the photo. Reveal a more interesting world latent in the photo's own structure.

## Hierarchy

- One hero object, one supporting apparatus gesture, one quiet field.
- The hero must read in one second at thumbnail size.
- Favor broad, legible forms over decorative fragments and dense mechanics.
- The apparatus frames, circulates, lifts, cradles, or releases the hero; it never competes with it.

## Color

- Clear, fresh, luminous, and controlled—not dark, muddy, neon-rainbow, or red-blue by default.
- Use four roles at most: field, hero, secondary, signal.
- Derive the palette from the object's own appetizing or emotional colors, then refine saturation and value relationships.
- Good accents include tea green, candied coral, milk white, mist blue, soft lilac, butter yellow, and one acid-lime or electric-cyan signal.

## Material

- Preserve the hero's essential tactile appeal.
- Choose two dominant materials and at most one accent: pearl ceramic, translucent resin, satin aluminum, soft enamel, liquid membrane, frosted acrylic, cloudy gel, paper, pastry, foam, or fabric.
- Transparent and metallic surfaces are accents, not automatic defaults.
- Keep a designed contrast between edible/soft/human and precise/artificial/spatial.

## Light and Finish

- Bright diffuse daylight or a soft studio field, physically coherent shadows, restrained glow.
- Premium C4D-like clarity without sterile product-render emptiness.
- Avoid dark cyberpunk, harsh bloom, dirty grunge, excessive reflections, and cheap plastic.

## Source Fidelity

- Preserve the object's silhouette, identifying details, and emotional appeal.
- Every major mechanism must originate in a visible source element.
- A successful result feels like the object revealed its private ritual.
