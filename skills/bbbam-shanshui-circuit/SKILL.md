# B.B.BAM Shanshui Circuit

Turn an ordinary place into one clear, enterable artificial shanshui world. Derive every new structure from visible source geometry; never decorate the photo with unrelated Chinese symbols or amusement props.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the source before proposing a style.
2. Identify one hero mass, one route candidate, one main void or water plane, and at most two supporting structures.
3. Build a source-to-world mapping. Every generated element must name its source counterpart.
4. Choose a transformation concept and material family using `references/transformation-rules.md`.
5. Generate or edit the image with the strongest available image-generation tool.
6. Review the result against the quality gate. If it reads as a filter, a generic glass future world, or a decorated landscape, revise it.
7. Deliver the source and result as a visible before/after pair. Briefly state what became the circuit and what was intentionally preserved.

## Non-Negotiable Composition

- Keep one dominant spatial idea: one route moving through one world.
- Preserve the source camera angle, horizon logic, major silhouettes, and identifiable place structure.
- Make the transformation substantial: rebuild spatial relationships, not merely color-grade surfaces.
- Let the route originate from an existing road, railing, shoreline, river, ridge, shadow edge, or building line.
- Use contemporary Chinese spatial logic—layered depth, borrowed scenery, void/solid rhythm, winding approach—not literal pavilions, lanterns, calligraphy, dragons, or random flowers.
- Use no more than three material families and four color roles.
- Keep a strong hero and generous quiet space. Avoid scattered miniature rides and dense ornament.

## Visual Direction

Read `references/visual-system.md` for the shared B.B.BAM aesthetic and `references/transformation-rules.md` for source mapping, material selection, prompting, and rejection criteria. Use `references/examples.md` when presenting or testing the Skill.

## Quality Gate

Approve only when all are true:

- The source place is still recognizable without needing the before image.
- At least one source structure has become a visibly functional circuit.
- The image has one immediate focal hierarchy at thumbnail size.
- The world feels fresh, clear, emotional, and designed—not dark, mystical, cluttered, or cheap.
- Added forms can be traced to source forms.
- The result is not interchangeable with the Offering Machine or a generic transparent-glass preset.

Reject and revise when the output is only retouched, visually noisy, dominated by chrome/glass, overly literal in its Chinese references, or filled with invented flora and architecture.
