# Operational Example: Green Overlook to Reservoir Garden

## Inputs

- Source: a casual vertical landscape photo with a large sky, distant ridge, narrow water opening, and dense foreground vegetation.
- Positive result: a luminous translucent world with enlarged mountain shells, a central blue liquid core, transformed foreground growth systems, and strong near–middle–far drama.

## Source Anchors

1. distant horizontal ridge;
2. central water opening;
3. dense foreground growth rhythm;
4. broad open sky;
5. optional directional edge.

## Good Transformation

- ridge → 1–2 enlarged enclosing shell systems;
- water opening → one saturated reservoir core with a few broad currents;
- foreground vegetation → 3–7 continuous translucent growth masses, several smooth domes, and a limited family of substantial columns;
- open sky → luminous atmospheric void;
- directional edge → optional wrapped contour only when circulation matters.

Transform more than the source while preserving lineage through relationships—not literal leaves or exact coordinates.

## Why the Positive Result Works

- recognizable ancestry without retouching;
- meaningful changes in scale, grouping, and spatial role;
- one central emotional core;
- rich near/middle/far layering;
- premium clear light and color;
- a coherent world thesis.

## Keep These Qualities

- shell-mountain drama;
- saturated liquid core;
- organized growth colony;
- smooth large domes;
- layered depth;
- strong foreground weight;
- contemporary luminous shanshui mood.

## Correct Only the Microtexture

Do not reduce the invention. Consolidate the fine detail:

- leaf-by-leaf interiors → broad translucent green veils, continuous canopy bodies, or a few large soft silhouettes;
- hundreds of thin reeds → a limited family of thicker smooth columns with clear spacing;
- water glitter → 2–5 long reflection bands or one coherent current;
- scattered highlights → a few long controlled highlights;
- cellular or pebble breakup → broad curvature and connected strata.

Keep macro and meso complexity. Remove only the fourth micro-detail scale.

## Failure Comparisons

Reject both extremes:

- **Too literal:** original landscape composition recolored with transparent material; reads as a filter.
- **Too empty:** world-building removed in the name of smoothness; loses the foreground system and emotional payoff.
- **Too granular:** ambitious world remains, but domes contain thousands of leaf edges and the water contains sparkling confetti.
- **Balanced:** bold new world, source-derived relationships, rich organized depth, and broad clean surfaces with only sparse long accents.

## Approve Only When

- the result is clearly a new world;
- selected anchors remain traceable;
- transformation operations are obvious;
- only three detail scales are visible;
- no region becomes leafy, granular, stippled, thorny, or glittery at thumbnail size;
- optional motifs have an actual conceptual role.
